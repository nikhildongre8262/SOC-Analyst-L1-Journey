# Day 12 - Introduction to Logs

## 1. What Are Logs?

Logs are **time-stamped records of activities, events, and errors** generated by systems, applications, devices, and security tools.

They help analysts understand what happened, when it happened, and—when the record contains that information—who or what was involved.

## 2. Why Do Logs Matter?

Logs are important in cybersecurity because they help us:

### Track Activity
Reconstruct activity and understand what happened, when it happened, and by whom.

### Identify Threats
Spot suspicious events, unusual behavior, and potential security incidents early.

### Support Investigations
Provide evidence for incident response, investigation, and digital forensics.

### Meet Compliance Requirements
Support auditing, reporting, and regulatory requirements.

---

## 3. Common Types of Logs

| Log Type | What It Records | Example Use in Security |
|---|---|---|
| **System Logs** | Operating system and system events | Investigate system errors, startup events, or service changes |
| **Security Logs** | Security-related events, such as logons and access attempts | Investigate failed logins or suspicious account activity |
| **Application Logs** | Events generated by software and applications | Troubleshoot application errors or unusual application behavior |
| **Network Logs** | Network activity recorded by devices and services | Review connections, firewall activity, DNS requests, or traffic patterns |
| **Cloud Logs** | Activity recorded by cloud services and platforms | Investigate cloud sign-ins, resource changes, and access activity |

> Log contents vary depending on the system, application, device, and logging configuration.

---

## 4. Why Logs Matter in Cybersecurity

Logs are more than just data—they help analysts build a timeline and understand the story behind activity.

### Detect Security Incidents
Logs can reveal suspicious activity, failed login attempts, unexpected access, and other indicators that may require investigation.

### Enable Threat Hunting
Analysts can search and correlate logs to find hidden patterns, suspicious behavior, and indicators of compromise that may not have triggered an alert.

### Support Digital Forensics
Logs provide evidence that can help investigators determine what happened, when it happened, and how an incident may have occurred.

### Meet Compliance Requirements
Organizations use logs to support security audits, monitoring, reporting, and standards or regulatory requirements such as PCI DSS and HIPAA, where applicable.

### Correlate Events and Gain Visibility
SIEM tools collect and correlate logs from multiple sources, helping analysts connect events and gain a broader view of activity across an environment.

---

## 5. Real-Time Monitoring and Dwell Time

- **Real-time monitoring:** Reviewing or alerting on events as they are generated or received, with minimal delay.
- **Dwell time:** The period an attacker remains in an environment before being detected or removed.

Timely log monitoring and investigation can help reduce detection delays and potentially shorten an attacker's dwell time.

---

## 6. How Logs Fit into a SOC

A simplified workflow:

1. Systems, endpoints, applications, network devices, and cloud services generate logs.
2. Logs are collected and forwarded to a central platform.
3. A SIEM (Security Information and Event Management) platform searches, analyzes, and correlates events.
4. Alerts or suspicious patterns are reviewed by SOC analysts.
5. Analysts investigate, document evidence, and escalate or respond according to procedures.

---

## Key Takeaway

> **Logs reveal what other security controls or people may miss.**

They give SOC analysts visibility, context, and evidence to detect suspicious activity and investigate incidents.

---

*Learning note: Log analysis becomes more effective when timestamps, source and destination details, usernames, event IDs, severity, and surrounding events are examined together—not in isolation.*
