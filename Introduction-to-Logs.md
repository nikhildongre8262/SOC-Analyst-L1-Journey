Introduction to Logs
Topics: Log types, formats, and importance in cybersecurity
Key idea: Logs are more than just data — they tell the story of what happened.

1. What Are Logs?
Logs are time-stamped records of activities, events, and errors generated by systems, applications, devices, and security tools.

They help an analyst understand:
- What happened?
- When did it happen?
- Where did it happen?
- Who or what was involved?
2. Why Do Logs Matter?
Logs are useful in cybersecurity because they help us:
Track Activity
See what happened, when it happened, and by whom.
Identify Threats
Spot suspicious or unusual behaviour early.
Support Investigations
Provide evidence for incident response and digital forensics.
Meet Compliance Requirements
Support auditing, reporting, and regulatory requirements.
3. Common Types of Logs
Log type	What it records (general examples)
System Logs	Operating-system and system events, errors, and service activity
Security Logs	Authentication, access, security events, and policy-related activity
Application Logs	Events, errors, and activity generated by applications
Network Logs	Network-device and network-traffic-related events, such as firewall or connection records
Cloud Logs	Activity and events from cloud services, resources, and accounts


4. Why Logs Matter in Cybersecurity / SOC
Detect Security Incidents
Identify suspicious activity, failed logins, unusual events, or potentially unauthorized access.
Enable Threat Hunting
Help analysts search for hidden patterns, indicators, and suspicious behaviour that may not have triggered an alert.
Support Digital Forensics
Provide evidence that helps investigators reconstruct what happened and understand how an incident occurred.
Meet Compliance Requirements
Logs can support standards, audits, and regulatory requirements such as ISO 27001, PCI DSS, HIPAA, and applicable regulations.
Correlate Events and Gain Visibility
SIEM tools can combine logs from multiple sources to provide a broader picture of activity across an environment.
5. Log Timeline: Live Activity and Dwell Time
Logs are time-stamped, so analysts can arrange events into a timeline.
- Live / real-time logs: Can help an analyst notice activity as it occurs or shortly after it occurs.
- Dwell time: The period an attacker remains undetected in an environment. Reviewing logs over time can help investigators understand activity before detection and estimate how long suspicious access may have persisted.
6. SOC Analyst Perspective
When reviewing a log, begin by asking:
- When? What is the timestamp?
- Who? Which user, account, process, or device is involved?
- Where? Which host, IP address, application, or resource?
- What? What event or action was recorded?
- Is it normal? Does it match expected activity, or is it unusual?
- What else happened? Can other logs help confirm or add context?
A single log entry may not prove an incident. Correlating multiple events and sources helps build a more reliable picture.
Key Takeaway
Logs reveal what others might miss. They turn system activity into evidence and help SOC analysts detect, investigate, and understand security incidents.

Quick Revision
- Logs = time-stamped records of activity.
- They are generated by systems, applications, devices, and security tools.
- Logs help track activity, identify threats, investigate incidents, and support compliance.
- Common categories: system, security, application, network, and cloud logs.
- SIEM tools correlate logs from multiple sources for improved visibility.
